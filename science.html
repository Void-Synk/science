6.1 Climate Change: A Global Challenge

What is Climate Change?

Climate is the average weather pattern of a specific region over a long period, typically 30 years.

Climate Change refers to a significant and lasting change in these statistical weather patterns over decades or longer.

While Earth's climate has changed naturally throughout history, current warming is unprecedented in speed due to human activity.

Causes of Climate Change

A. Natural Causes

Solar Activities

Changes in the sun's energy output directly influence Earth's climate balance.

Earth's Reflectivity (Albedo)

Ice and snow reflect sunlight back to space. As ice melts, darker ocean/land surfaces absorb more sunlight, accelerating warming.

Volcanic Eruptions

Eruptions release aerosols that temporarily cool the planet, but also emit greenhouse gases contributing to long-term warming.

Causes of Climate Change

B. Human-Induced Causes

Burning Fossil Fuels

Combustion of coal, oil, and gas for energy, transport, and industry releases massive CO2 emissions.

Industrialization

Cement, steel, and plastic production rely heavily on fossil fuels, releasing major pollutants.

Deforestation

Forests act as natural "carbon sinks". Cutting trees destroys carbon storage and releases stored CO2.

Agriculture & Livestock

Unsustainable farming practices and livestock rearing release potent greenhouse gases like methane (CH4).

Global Impacts

Effects of Climate Change

Extreme Weather Events

Increased frequency of severe droughts, flash floods, and unseasonal storms causing crop failures.

Rising Sea Levels

Thermal ocean expansion and melting polar glaciers threaten coastal cities and low-lying islands.

Loss of Biodiversity

Species unable to adapt face extinction risk, disrupting ecosystem stability.

Health & Economic Costs

Rise in vector-borne diseases, heatstrokes, malnutrition, and billions lost in damaged infrastructure.

Local Evidence

Evidence in Nepal

Nepal contributes minimally to global GHG emissions, yet remains highly vulnerable to climate impacts.

Rapid Temperature Rise

Average temperature is increasing at approximately 0.06°C per year.

Drying Natural Water Sources

Rivers, lakes, and freshwater springs are rapidly drying up.

GLOF Risk

Glacial Lake Outburst Floods pose immediate catastrophic danger to downstream communities.

Mountain Snow Depletion

Rapid snow and ice melt are reducing the height and snowlines of Himalayan peaks.

Geography-Specific Vulnerabilities

Impacts Across Nepal's Regions

Mountain Region

  • Accelerated glacial melting and glacier retreat.
  • High risk of Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs).
  • Disruption to alpine ecology and trekking tourism.

Hills Region

  • Frequent rainfall-induced landslides and soil erosion.
  • Changing crop cultivation patterns and dates.
  • Drying natural drinking springs and traditional wells.

Terai Region

  • Severe river flooding and prolonged drought spells.
  • Extreme heatwaves affecting agricultural labor.
  • Habitats loss threatening lowland wildlife species.
Control and Mitigation Measures

Individual & Personal Action

Reduce Carbon Emissions

Walk or cycle for short journeys. Utilize public transit. Turn off lights and idle electronics.

Emphasize Carbon Storage

Plant trees across school compounds. Actively participate in community afforestation campaigns.

Behavioral Change

Practice 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle), avoid single-use plastics, conserve water, and try weekly plant-based diets.

Policy & Research Focus

Global Measures & Case Study

Renewable Energy Transition

Transitioning energy grids to hydro, solar, and wind while improving industrial energy efficiency.

Enforcing Forest Policies

Enforcing strict legal protections for national forests and supporting climate-resilient agriculture.

Case Study: Imja Glacial Lake

Imja Lake in the Everest region serves as a prime example of GLOF mitigation efforts, where engineering lowering projects reduced water levels to protect downstream settlements.
